# Settings

[← Documentation index](README.md) · [Source index](source-index.md)

The defaults are the settings the author plays with: texture detail low, light levels medium, interlaced on, draw distance 512, crank speed 1.4, max framerate 30, show FPS on (plus Quake's own defaults). An existing config.cfg keeps whatever it says; "Reset defaults" goes back to these.

The Options menu (drawn with a double-size font; the Customize controls, Go to console, Screen size, Invert mouse, Lookspring and Lookstrafe rows are gone, so the console can no longer be opened on the device; `viewsize` and the others keep whatever config.cfg says) keeps its settings (brightness, **crank speed**, **music** on/off, volume, always run, autofire, **texture detail**, **light levels**, **interlaced**, **dithering**, **draw distance**, **max framerate**, **show FPS** ...) in `config.cfg` in the game's Data folder. It is written when you leave the Options menu and when the system pauses, locks or terminates the game, and read at the next launch. Key bindings are not saved (they come from `default.cfg` and the port's own button mapping).

The Load and Save menus use the same double-size font. A slot shows the level name cut to 15 characters and the kills as `killed/total` (the full 39-character save comment does not fit at that size).

Texture detail: `low` (default, `d_mipcap 1`, the sharpest mip level is never used; about 1.6 ms per frame faster in the demos, about 3%, with visibly softer textures) or `high` (`d_mipcap 0`).

Light levels: `lowest`, `low`, `medium` (default) or `high` (`r_lightlevels` 4, 8, 16 or 64; left/right step through them without wrapping around). How many shades of light the walls, floors and ceilings get. `high` draws them as before: Quake's 64 shades, with the texture perspective-correct every 8 pixels. The other three are faster:

- They use only 4, 8 or 16 of the 64 shades. Looking up a lit texel goes through Quake's 16 KB colour map, one 256-byte row per shade, and a frame that uses all 64 rows keeps missing the small data cache; with fewer rows in use most lookups hit. The shades kept are the ones that look most different on the Playdate's screen: the display stretches Quake's dark picture and clips its bright end to white, so the bright shades look nearly alike there and the dark ones differ a lot. The kept shades are spread evenly in that on-screen brightness, from the brightest to black, so they fall mostly in the dark half, where the game's pixels are; every other shade uses the nearest kept one (`lowest` keeps shades 0, 37, 52 and 63).
- The textures are mapped PSX-style: the texture position is exact at the two ends of each row of a surface and stepped linearly in between, instead of every 8 pixels, so textures bend a little on long walls and floors seen at a steep angle. The light is still sampled every 16 pixels along the row.

Measured on the device over the three demos (3D world drawing, compared with `high` in the same run): `medium` saves 1.1-1.6 ms per frame, `low` 1.2-1.8 ms and `lowest` 1.3-2.0 ms, about 1-1.7 ms of the whole frame (3-5%). `medium` and `low` look close to `high` apart from the bending; `lowest` posterizes lit walls visibly. With the `patterns` dithering a dark room has only five grey levels per 2x2 block, so there a small shift in light can turn a block of dots black; such patches show up most at `low` and `lowest`. Models, sprites, particles, water and sky are not affected.

Interlaced: `on` (default, `r_interlace 1`) or `off` (`r_interlace 0`). When on, each frame draws only every other row of the 3D view (alternating odd and even rows), and the other rows keep the previous frame, so moving edges comb slightly. World spans, sky, alias models, sprites and particles all skip the kept rows, and the display does not re-dither them either. The edge scan, world traversal and surface cache builds still run in full, so the gain is less than half: demo1 44.5 -> 39.2 ms per frame on the device (-12%, scan 16.0 -> 11.6 ms). A resized view or a new map always gets one full frame.

Dithering: how the 8-bit picture becomes the 1-bit display (`pd_dither`, `0`-`3`, default `0`; left/right or A cycle through the modes and wrap around). The 3D view is rendered at half resolution, so the first mode has a cheaper shortcut for it:

- `patterns` (default, `pd_dither 0`): every 2x2 block of the 3D view becomes one of five fixed patterns (black, one white pixel, half, one black pixel, white), several equally bright variants per palette colour ramp so different surfaces stay apart. Everything else (HUD, menus, console, blocks that something was drawn over) is dithered per pixel with a 4x4 Bayer matrix. It is the cheapest mode and looks the cleanest, but it has only five grey levels, so dim areas drop to black.
- `bayer` (`pd_dither 1`): the 4x4 Bayer matrix for every pixel, the 3D view included: 17 grey levels, a regular grid pattern, and the dim surfaces that `patterns` loses are visible.
- `blue noise` (`pd_dither 2`): a 32x32 blue noise tile for every pixel (256 grey levels): an even grain with no grid, at the price of a rougher HUD and menu. The tile comes from `scripts/gen-bluenoise.py` (void-and-cluster; `port/boards/playdate/bluenoise.h`).
- `diffusion` (`pd_dither 3`): Floyd-Steinberg error diffusion with a random threshold, for the 3D view only (the HUD, menus and console keep the Bayer dither, and so does any row pair of the view that something was drawn over, so text stays readable). Each pixel is white when its luminance plus the error carried to it is above a threshold drawn at random from 64-191 (a hash of the pixel position, so the grain does not flicker), and what it got wrong is spread over the pixel to its right and the three below it. Error diffusion keeps the average brightness whatever the threshold is, so a threshold that follows the scene on its own would change little; the scene's median luminance (sampled from the 3D view once per frame, smoothed and with hysteresis) therefore also drives a tone curve that stretches it to the middle grey the thresholds are centred on. Half of the pixels of a dark or a bright scene end up on either side, so dark rooms keep their detail instead of dropping to black, at the price of a brighter and grainier picture. Each pair of LCD rows is diffused on its own, top row into bottom row, and the scan direction alternates from pair to pair, so interlaced frames can keep rows and the picture is the same whatever is redrawn.

The per-pixel modes compare every LCD pixel with its threshold instead of looking up a pattern per block, which costs more CPU in the display step than `patterns` (estimated at 1-1.5 ms per frame for `bayer` and `blue noise` when every row of the 3D view is drawn, about half of that interlaced). `diffusion` costs more again: about 12 instructions per pixel in the inner loop (counted in the ARM code), an estimated 4-6 ms per frame when every row of the 3D view is drawn and about half of that interlaced. None of this is measured on a device. Changing the mode redraws the whole picture, including the rows an interlaced frame keeps. A build without `PD_LOWRES_3D` has no patterns and no 3D view to follow, so `patterns` and `diffusion` are the same as `bayer` there.

Crank speed: degrees of view turn per degree of crank (`crank_speed`, 0.2-3 in steps of 0.2, default 1.4). It replaces the Mouse Speed slider.

Draw distance: a slider over 256, 384, 512, 768, 1024, 1536, 2048, 3072 and unlimited (the right end; `r_maxdist` in Quake units, default 512, 0 = unlimited). World leaves whose bounding box is entirely beyond it are not drawn (except their sky, so the sky still shows behind them), and neither are models, brush entities or particles beyond it; what is culled is drawn black (the background surface uses palette index 0 instead of `r_clearcolor` while a distance is set). A face is still drawn while any near leaf holds it, so the cut follows leaf boundaries rather than a clean line. demo1 is mostly indoors: 768 saves about 0.5 ms per frame there (40.2 -> 39.7); open areas gain more.

Show FPS: `on` (default) or `off` (`scr_showfps`): the frame-rate counter in the top right corner of the screen. It counts the frames over half a second and redraws its few characters at that rate, so it costs next to nothing.

Max framerate: 30, 50 or unlimited (`pd_maxfps`, 0 = unlimited): the rate the Playdate calls the game at (`display->setRefreshRate`). The default is 30; the build's `PD_REFRESH_RATE` only applies while the game loads, until the settings are read. A change applies on the next frame, also from the console or config.cfg. Above 30 the battery drains faster, and a higher cap only helps in scenes the game renders in under 33 ms (50) or 20 ms (unlimited).

Under water, slime and lava Quake blends the whole palette 50-60% towards the liquid's colour. On the 1-bit display only the luminance survives, so that blend just squeezed the picture into the midtones (under water black became mid grey and the highlights clipped to white). The port leaves the liquid tint out of the palette (`V_UpdatePalette` in `winquake/view.c`), so the view keeps the full black-to-white range; the underwater warp, and the damage, pickup and powerup flashes, are unchanged.
